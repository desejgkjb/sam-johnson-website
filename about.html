In February 2011, a devastating earthquake struck Christchurch. Sam Johnson was a university student watching his city break apart — and watching thousands of young people being told to stay out of the way. He disagreed. Using Facebook, he called on students to grab shovels and help dig the city out of liquefaction silt.

Eleven thousand volunteers answered. The Student Volunteer Army was born — proof that young people, given trust and a practical task, will show up in extraordinary numbers.

In the years since, Sam has carried that lesson into disaster zones in Japan, the Philippines, Vanuatu and Nepal, and into classrooms, boardrooms and parliaments across the Pacific, Saudi Arabia and Brazil. His work has been recognised with the Young New Zealander of the Year award, Communicator of the Year, and the Sir Peter Blake Leadership Award.

His vision remains the same as it was in 2011: to catalyse a revolution in youth leadership.
